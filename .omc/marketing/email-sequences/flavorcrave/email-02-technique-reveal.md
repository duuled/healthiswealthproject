## What the layering method actually means

When your grandmother added spices wasn't random. It was instinct built over decades. And that instinct followed a pattern most home cooks never learn because nobody teaches it explicitly:

**Spices behave differently depending on when and where you add them.**

Here's the breakdown:

**1. Bloom your dry spices in fat first.**
Before you add anything else to the pan — onion, garlic, protein — add your dry spices directly to the hot oil. 30 to 60 seconds. This releases the fat-soluble compounds that carry flavor. What you're smelling when you do this right is the food waking up.

**2. Build your aromatics on top of that base.**
Onion, garlic, celery, bell pepper — whatever your base is — goes in after the spices have bloomed. They pick up all that flavor and carry it into everything else in the pot.

**3. Season in layers, not all at once.**
A pinch early. A pinch in the middle. A taste and adjustment at the end. Not one heavy-handed dump at the finish. Each layer has time to integrate.

**4. Know which spices are heat-sensitive.**
Fresh herbs and bright spices (cilantro, fresh thyme, lemon zest) go in last. Bold, earthy ones (cumin, smoked paprika, allspice) need heat to open up. Treat them differently.
