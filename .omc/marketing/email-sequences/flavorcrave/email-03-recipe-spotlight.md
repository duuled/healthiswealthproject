In our house — and I'm guessing in yours too — Sunday dinner wasn't just a meal. It was a performance. People had roles. There was the person who made the greens. The person who handled the meat. The person trusted with the potato salad. And then there was the mac and cheese.

The mac and cheese had weight. If it was good, people talked about it for weeks. If it was off — too runny, not enough crust, wrong cheese — they'd be polite about it to your face and then whisper about it in the car ride home. You know exactly what I'm talking about.

For years I chased my grandmother's version. I tried boxed stuff gussied up with sharp cheddar. I tried stovetop. I tried recipes from food blogs. Some were fine. None of them hit the way hers did.

---

## What I finally figured out

Here's what I learned after years of testing:

**1. The cheese ratio matters more than the cheese list.**
Sharp cheddar is non-negotiable for flavor. But if you don't balance it with something creamier — a good Gruyere or a mild Colby — it'll break when it bakes. The sharpness makes it taste right. The creamy cheese makes it hold.

**2. The egg custard is what separates baked mac from casserole.**
A lot of recipes skip this or gloss over it. The eggs go into your milk and cheese mixture and they set up during baking, giving you that dense, sliceable texture instead of a loose scoop. Don't skip the eggs.

**3. The top layer is its own thing.**
You want the top to crust. That means it needs to be exposed. Not covered in foil. Not buried under breadcrumbs. Just the cheese, the heat, and time.

**4. Rest it.**
Pull it out of the oven and let it sit for at least 10 minutes before you cut it. The custard needs to set. This is where a lot of people lose the battle — they cut it too early and it falls apart.
