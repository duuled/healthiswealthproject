## Prompt 1: Client Proposal Writer

Use this when you need to send a project proposal and you're staring at a blank document.

```
You are a professional freelance [YOUR ROLE — e.g., copywriter, designer, consultant].

Write a project proposal for the following:

Client: [CLIENT NAME OR TYPE]
Project: [BRIEF DESCRIPTION — e.g., "a 5-page website redesign for a law firm"]
My rate: [YOUR RATE — e.g., "$150/hr" or "$2,500 flat"]
Timeline: [ESTIMATED TIMELINE]
Key deliverables: [LIST 3-5 DELIVERABLES]

Structure the proposal with these sections: Project Overview, Scope of Work, Deliverables, Timeline, Investment, and Next Steps.

Keep the tone professional but warm. Write in first person. Do not use jargon or filler language. End with a clear call to action asking the client to confirm they'd like to move forward.
```

**What this replaces:** 45 minutes of staring at a blank page and second-guessing your phrasing.

---

## Prompt 2: Meeting Notes to Action Items

Use this immediately after any call where you took even rough notes.

```
Here are my raw notes from a meeting:

[PASTE YOUR NOTES — bullet points, fragments, whatever you have]

Do the following:
1. Summarize the meeting in 3 sentences or less
2. Extract all action items, who owns each one, and any deadlines mentioned
3. List any open questions that weren't resolved
4. Note any decisions that were made

Format the output as a clean, copy-pasteable summary I can send to participants.
```

**What this replaces:** 30 minutes of trying to turn your chicken-scratch notes into something you'd actually send to a client.

---

## Prompt 3: Blog Post from Bullet Points

Use this when you know what you want to say but don't want to write the full thing from scratch.

```
Turn these bullet points into a complete, publish-ready blog post:

Topic: [YOUR TOPIC]
Target audience: [WHO YOU'RE WRITING FOR]
Tone: [e.g., "conversational and direct, like talking to a peer"]
Word count target: [e.g., 800-1000 words]

Bullet points:
- [POINT 1]
- [POINT 2]
- [POINT 3]
- [POINT 4]
- [POINT 5]

Include a compelling intro that leads with a hook, subheadings for each main section, and a closing paragraph with a clear takeaway. Do not pad the word count with filler. Every sentence should earn its place.
```

**What this replaces:** Writing from scratch, then editing, then second-guessing whether it sounds like you wrote it.
