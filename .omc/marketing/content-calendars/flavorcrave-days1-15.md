## Day 6 — Science of Soul Food: Low and Slow Heat

**TikTok/Reels**
- Hook: "You're cooking your collard greens wrong. Here's why they're still bitter."
- Angle: Split-screen showing collard greens rushed on high heat (still tough, still bitter) versus the low-and-slow method (dark, silky, tender). Voiceover explains the science: heat breaks down the cell walls and drives out the bitterness over time, which is why your grandma cooked them for two hours minimum.
- Caption: Bitterness in greens isn't a flaw, it's a chemistry problem. Cook them fast and hot and that bitterness stays. Cook them low and slow and the heat does the work — breaking down the oxalic acid, tenderizing the leaves, letting the smoked meat get into every layer. That's the science behind Sunday dinner. ## Day 12 — Science of Soul Food: Seasoning in Layers

**TikTok/Reels**
- Hook: "Your food tastes flat because you're seasoning it all at the end."
- Angle: Demonstration video showing two pots cooking simultaneously. One gets all seasoning dumped in at the end. The other gets seasoned at every stage — the oil, the aromatics, the protein, the liquid. Final taste comparison. The layered version wins by a mile.
- Caption: Seasoning isn't a step at the end. It's a process through the whole cook. Season the oil. Season when you add the aromatics. Season the protein before it hits the pan. Season the liquid. Taste and adjust before you plate. That's how grandma's food always tasted different from yours — she seasoned in layers without thinking about it. That's the technique. More at theflavorcrave.com.
