## Brand Voice & Tone

### Voice Pillars
1. **Local Authority** — We know LA block-by-block. We name streets, not just neighborhoods.
2. **Anti-Tourist** — We call out tourist traps by name. We celebrate hidden spots. No fluff.
3. **Culture-Forward** — Food is culture. Immigrant-owned, community-rooted spots get the spotlight they deserve.
4. **Brutally Honest** — If it's overpriced or overhyped, we say so. Our credibility is our product.
5. **Accessible** — No food snobbery. Great food is for everyone, at every price point.

### Tone by Context
- **TikTok:** Walk-up style, street-level, no studio. React in real time. Show the line.
- **Blog / Guides:** Specific, opinionated, neighborhood-by-neighborhood. Link to Google Maps.
- **Pinterest:** SEO-optimized titles, beautiful food photography descriptions, save-worthy roundups.
- **Newsletter:** Friend who texts you the best spots. Casual, direct, one clear recommendation per issue.

### Voice DON'Ts
- Never write vague praise: "amazing ambiance," "great vibes" — be specific
- Never avoid calling out overpriced or tourist-trap spots by name
- Never ignore neighborhood context (who lives there, what the food represents)
- Never write from a white-gaze food perspective — center the cultures behind the food
- Never include a restaurant without a specific dish recommendation and price

## Target Audience

### Primary ICP
- **Age:** 22–38, LA locals
- **Identity:** Culture-curious, diverse, always looking for the next great spot
- **Income:** $35K–$80K — they care about value, not just price
- **Psychographic:** "I'm tired of Yelp, tired of Eater LA, tired of the same 10 spots. Tell me where the real LA eats."
- **Behavior:** Saves TikToks for food recs, uses Instagram for food discovery, shares spots with friend groups

- Tourists who want to eat like locals (high search volume)

## Content Pillars

1. **Neighborhood Deep-Dives** (35%) — Complete food guides per neighborhood. "Everything you need to eat in [neighborhood], ranked."
2. **Honest vs Overhyped** (25%) — Call out tourist traps. "Eater LA is wrong about this." Counter-programming to mainstream food media.
3. **Price Tier Content** (20%) — "Broke / Budget / Baller" format. Three spots per cuisine per neighborhood at each price tier.
4. **"Best [cuisine] in LA"** (20%) — SEO anchor pages. Tacos, ramen, Korean BBQ, soul food, birria, jerk chicken, etc.

### SEO Content Format
Every guide must have:
- Keyword in H1 (natural)
- Google Maps embed or link per restaurant

- "What to order" per spot (specific dish + price)

- Schema markup: LocalBusiness + ItemList

## Viral Content Frameworks

### "Tourist Trap vs Local Spot"
Hook: "Every tourist goes to [famous spot]. Every local goes here."
Format: Side-by-side comparison. Price. Wait time. Quality. Verdict.

### "The $[X] Challenge"
Hook: "I ate in [neighborhood] for $20. Here's what I got."
Format: Show every item. Receipts visible. React in real time.

### "Neighborhood Tier List"
Hook: "I ranked every LA neighborhood by food quality. This will be controversial."
Format: S/A/B/C tiers on screen. Defend each choice.

### "First Week in LA"
Hook: "POV: You just moved to LA. Don't eat at any of the places your coworkers recommend."
Format: 5 spots in 60 seconds. Address + dish + price on screen.
